Diminished passing tones for the minor 7th

Harmonise the passing tones of the minor 7th as diminished chords.

The minor 7th bebop scale is composed of chord tones matching Dm7 (D–F–A–C) and non-chord tones (E, G, B♭ and C♯). Just as in the sixth-diminished scale, the non-chord tones spell a diminished seventh chord. This chord can be considered the dominant of D minor, a rootless A7♭9 chord.

So whenever the melody hits a passing tone, voice it as A7♭9 with the melody on top; when it returns to a chord tone, go back to Dm7.

The two chords alternate on every step, and the diminished chord pulls back into Dm7 the same way A7♭9 resolves to D minor.

With C♯ spelled as D♭, the same four notes are also a rootless C7♭9, the dominant of F6. One diminished chord leads home to both Dm7 and F6, which is why the pairing of a sixth chord with its diminished neighbour from Part 1 works unchanged on a minor 7th chord.

Exercise Minor 7th bebop scale with block chords, ascending and descending

Play the minor 7th bebop scale on D up and back down in eighth notes, one block chord per note: Dm7 on the chord tones and A7♭9 on every passing tone.

The goal is to switch between the Dm7 and A7♭9 shapes without stopping, in both directions.

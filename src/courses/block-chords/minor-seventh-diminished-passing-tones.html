Diminished passing tones for the minor 7th

Harmonise the passing tones of the minor 7th as diminished chords.

The D Dorian group of 7 is composed of chord tones matching Dm7 (D–F–A–C) and three non-chord tones: E, G and B. Unlike the passing tones of the sixth-diminished scale, these three don't all belong to one diminished seventh chord, so we need two.

E and G belong to the diminished chord E–G–B♭–C♯. This chord can be considered the dominant of D minor, a rootless A7♭9 chord. B is not in that chord. It takes the diminished chord built on the root of Dm7 itself, D°7 (D–F–A♭–B), which keeps D and F where they are.

So whenever the melody hits a passing tone, voice it as a diminished chord with the melody on top, A7♭9 on E and G and D°7 on B; when it returns to a chord tone, go back to Dm7.

The chords alternate on every step. The first diminished chord pulls back into Dm7 the same way A7♭9 resolves to D minor.

The second one never leaves Dm7 behind: D and F stay in place, and on the way up B and A♭ slide a half step up to C and A.

Exercise Group of 7 with diminished passing tones, ascending and descending

Play the D Dorian group of 7 up to the seventh and back down in eighth notes, one block chord per note: Dm7 on the chord tones, A7♭9 on E and G, and D°7 on B.

The goal is to switch between the Dm7 and diminished shapes without stopping, in both directions.

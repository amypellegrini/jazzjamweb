Building the minor 7th block chords

In Part 1 every block was built around C6, the 1 chord of a 2 5 1 in C major. Part 2 moves to the 2 chord, Dm7, and takes it through the same steps: the block itself, the scale to practise it on, and the three ways to harmonise its passing tones.

The shape doesn't change. Play the melody note on top with the right hand, fill in three chord tones directly beneath it in close position, and double the melody an octave lower with the left hand. With D in the melody, the three chord tones beneath it are C, A and F.

A minor 7th chord is a sixth chord seen from another note. Dm7 (D–F–A–C) has the same four notes as F6 (F–A–C–D): start F6 from its sixth, D, and you are playing Dm7. So the Dm7 blocks are the very blocks you would play on F6, and everything you learned on C6 carries over.

Exercise Preparation for block chords scale in Dm7

Play all positions of the Dm7 block chord in ascending and descending order, one block per beat: the melody on D, F, A and C, up to the D an octave higher and back down again. The melody always doubled an octave lower in the left hand.

The minor 7th group of 7

In a 2 5 1 progression, the 2 chord uses the Dorian mode. In C major the 2 chord is Dm7, so it uses D Dorian: the notes of the C major scale, played from D.

To practise the minor 7th blocks we'll use a simple melodic pattern called a group of 7: the scale starting on the root and ascending to the seventh. For Dm7 that is D, E, F, G, A, B, C. Played in eighth notes, the four notes of Dm7 (D, F, A and C) land on the beat, and the three passing tones (E, G and B) fall on the upbeat. The same holds coming back down from the seventh, from C to D.

Why not a bebop scale, as in Part 1? Bebop scales work well in a context of fast-changing harmony and melodies, where the minor 7th chord is melodically collapsed with the dominant for convenience. That is not the case with block chords, where the vertical harmony is more prominent and chord tones are harmonised differently from non-chord tones. So Dm7 stays a chord in its own right, with its own mode.

As in Part 1, we can arrange it progressively, starting with the chord tones. Play the whole group of 7 with the left hand, and add the Dm7 block chords with the right hand only when playing a chord tone. On non-chord tones the right hand can remain silent.

Exercise Group of 7 in the left hand, up and down

Play the D Dorian group of 7 up to the seventh and back down in eighth notes with the left hand, and add the Dm7 block with the right hand on every chord tone, resting on the passing tones.

The goal is to land each Dm7 position on the beat while the left hand keeps the line moving, in both directions.

Chromatic passing tones for the minor 7th

Harmonise the passing tone with the target chord's own voicing, shifted by a half step.

The technique is the one from Part 1, and it still only works when approaching the target note chromatically. Going up the minor 7th bebop scale, two passing tones sit a half step below a chord tone: E and C♯. With F in the melody, the Dm7 voicing (A–C–D–F) can just as well be read as F6 (Dm7 over F). E leads to F, so it takes that voicing a half step lower: G♯–B–C♯–E, an E6. C♯ leads to D, so it takes the Dm7 voicing (F–A–C–D) a half step lower: E–G♯–B–C♯, a C♯m7. Each approach block then slides up into place, E6 into F6 and C♯m7 into Dm7. The other two passing tones, G and B♭, keep the C7 voicing of the previous lesson.

Coming down the scale, the approaches change direction. E no longer leads to a note a half step away, so it falls back to the rootless Dm9 voicing, E–F–A–C–E. D♭ now approaches C from above, so it takes C's voicing a half step higher. C is voiced D–F–A–C, a Dm7 over its seventh; a half step up, that becomes E♭–G♭–B♭–D♭, an E♭m7 over D♭, which slides down into place. B♭ works the same way: it sits a half step above A, so it takes A's voicing a half step higher. A is voiced C–D–F–A, a Dm7 over its fifth; a half step up, that becomes D♭–E♭–G♭–B♭, an E♭m7 over B♭.

Exercise Chromatic passing tones for the minor 7th, up and down

Play the minor 7th bebop scale on D up and back down in eighth notes, one block per note. Going up, E and C♯ approach F and D from a half step below, as E6 and C♯m7; coming down, D♭ and B♭ approach C and A from a half step above, as E♭m7/D♭ and E♭m7/B♭, and E falls back to the rootless Dm9. G takes C7 in both directions.

The goal is to feel how the approach chord changes with the direction of the melody.

Chromatic passing tones for the minor 7th

Harmonise the passing tone with the target chord's own voicing, shifted by a half step.

The technique is the one from Part 1, and it still only works when approaching the target note chromatically. Going up the D Dorian group of 7, two passing tones sit a half step below a chord tone: E leads to F, and B leads to C. Each takes its target's Dm7 voicing a half step lower, which makes it a C♯m7. F is voiced A–C–D–F, a Dm7 over its third; a half step down, that becomes G♯–B–C♯–E, a C♯m7 over E. C is voiced D–F–A–C, a Dm7 over its seventh; a half step down, that becomes C♯–E–G♯–B, a C♯m7 over B. Each approach block then slides up into place. G is a whole step below A, so it keeps the Em7 voicing of the previous lesson.

Coming back down, the technique has nothing to work with. B, G and E each sit a whole step above the chord tone they lead to, so none of them approaches its target chromatically. They all fall back to the diatonic voicings of the previous lesson: Em7 on B and G, and the rootless Dm9 on E.

Exercise Chromatic passing tones for the minor 7th, up and down

Play the D Dorian group of 7 up to the seventh and back down in eighth notes, one block per note. Going up, E and B approach F and C from a half step below, as C♯m7/E and C♯m7/B, and G takes Em7; coming down, B and G take Em7 and E takes the rootless Dm9.

The goal is to feel how the approach chord changes with the direction of the melody.

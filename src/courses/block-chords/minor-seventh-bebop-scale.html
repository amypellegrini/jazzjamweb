The minor 7th bebop scale

To practise the minor 7th blocks we need a scale that does for Dm7 what the sixth-diminished scale does for C6. Because Dm7 and F6 share their notes, it is the F sixth-diminished scale started from D: D, E, F, G, A, B♭, C, C♯ (or D♭). We'll call it the minor 7th bebop scale. Seen from D, it's the D natural minor scale with an extra semitone between C and D. With eight notes instead of seven, the scale lines up with the beat: played in eighth notes, the four notes of Dm7 (D, F, A and C) land on the beat, and the four passing tones (E, G, B♭ and C♯) fall on the upbeat.

In a 2 5 1 in C major you might expect B over Dm7, not B♭. The scale takes its B♭ from F6: with it, the four passing tones spell a single diminished seventh chord, which is what the next lesson builds on.

As in Part 1, we can arrange the scale progressively, starting with the chord tones. Play the whole scale with the left hand, and add the Dm7 block chords with the right hand only when playing a chord tone. On non-chord tones the right hand can remain silent.

Exercise Minor 7th bebop scale in the left hand, up and down

Play the minor 7th bebop scale on D up and back down in eighth notes with the left hand, and add the Dm7 block with the right hand on every chord tone, resting on the passing tones.

The goal is to land each Dm7 position on the beat while the left hand keeps the line moving, in both directions.

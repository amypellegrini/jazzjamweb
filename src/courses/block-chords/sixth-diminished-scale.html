Harmonising the sixth-diminished scale

To build and practise every technique, we'll use Barry Harris's sixth-diminished scale, also known as the major bebop scale. In C, it's the C major scale with an extra semitone between G and A (G♯ or A♭): C, D, E, F, G, A♭ (or G♯), A, B. With eight notes instead of seven, the scale lines up with the beat: played in eighth notes, the four notes of C6 (C, E, G and A) land on the beat, and the four passing tones (D, F, A♭ and B) fall on the upbeat.

We can arrange the C major sixth-diminished scale progressively, starting with the chord tones. Play the whole scale with the left hand, and add the C6 block chords with the right hand only when playing a chord tone. On non-chord tones the right hand can remain silent.

Exercise Scale in the left hand, up and down

Play the C bebop scale up and back down in eighth notes with the left hand, and add the C6 block with the right hand on every chord tone, resting on the passing tones.

The goal is to land each C6 position on the beat while the left hand keeps the line moving, in both directions.
